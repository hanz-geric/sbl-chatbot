## T01: Is DTI business name registration enough for me to start operating a business?

**Expected:** No. DTI registers only the business name; you also need a business (Mayor's) permit from the LGU and BIR registration.

**Tool:** search_knowledge_base (19.9 s)

No. DTI registration covers only your business name. You still need a business permit (Mayor’s Permit) from your city or municipality, and BIR registration. Please contact the Business Permits Office for more information.

**Sources:** 01.9 Common Questions – Business Registration; 02.8 Common questions – business permits; 01.2 DTI Business Name Registration (Sole Proprietorship) > Validity and renewal

## T02: How much does it cost to register a business name with DTI for city or municipal scope?

**Expected:** PHP 500 plus PHP 30 documentary stamp tax.

**Tool:** search_knowledge_base (14.5 s)

The registration fee is ₱500, plus ₱30 documentary stamp tax.

**Sources:** 01.2 DTI Business Name Registration (Sole Proprietorship) > Territorial scope and fees; 01.9 Common Questions – Business Registration; 01.9 Common Questions – Business Registration

## T03: How long is a DTI business name registration valid?

**Expected:** 5 years; renew after; 50% surcharge for late renewal.

**Tool:** search_knowledge_base (15.9 s)

A DTI business name registration is valid for 5 years from the date of registration. Please check with the City Planning and Development Office (CPDO) for business permit requirements.

**Sources:** 01.2 DTI Business Name Registration (Sole Proprietorship) > Validity and renewal; 01.2 DTI Business Name Registration (Sole Proprietorship) > Amendments; 02.8 Common questions – business permits

## T04: Who can register a business name with DTI?

**Expected:** Any Filipino at least 18 years old; foreign nationals authorized to do business may also register.

**Tool:** search_knowledge_base (12.0 s)

Any individual can register a business name with DTI through the Business Name Registration System (BNRS). DTI regional and provincial offices and Negosyo Centers can assist with online applications, renewals, and certification requests.

**Sources:** 02.8 Common questions – business permits; 01.9 Common Questions – Business Registration; 01.2 DTI Business Name Registration (Sole Proprietorship) > What it is

## T05: I have an online shop with only one office, but my customers are all over the Philippines. Do I need national scope with DTI?

**Expected:** Not necessarily; scope follows office/branch location, so one office in one barangay can use barangay scope (DTI office guidance).

**Tool:** search_knowledge_base (17.9 s)

According to DTI Baguio–Benguet, scope follows where your office or branches are located, not where your customers are. One office in one barangay can use barangay scope.

**Sources:** 01.2 DTI Business Name Registration (Sole Proprietorship) > Who can register; 01.2 DTI Business Name Registration (Sole Proprietorship) > Registering in person and special cases; 01.9 Common Questions – Business Registration

## T06: Can I transfer my DTI business name to my cousin?

**Expected:** No; business name is not transferable; must be cancelled first before someone else can register it.

**Tool:** search_knowledge_base (15.0 s)

No. A DTI business name is not transferable. The registered owner’s name must be cancelled before a new owner can register it. Please contact the DTI to cancel the existing name.

**Sources:** 01.2 DTI Business Name Registration (Sole Proprietorship) > Amendments; 02.8 Common questions – business permits; 01.2 DTI Business Name Registration (Sole Proprietorship) > Cancellation

## T10: What BIR form does a sole proprietor use to register, and what documents are needed?

**Expected:** BIR Form 1901; government ID; invoices (BIR Printed Invoices or sample from accredited printer); DTI certificate if applicable; PHP 30 DST.

**Tool:** search_knowledge_base (24.0 s)

A sole proprietor uses BIR Form 1901 to register. The required documents are:

*   BIR Form 1901 (two originals for manual applications)
*   One photocopy of a government-issued ID showing name, address, and birthdate. If the ID has no address, add proof of residence or business address.
*   Online applicants upload a selfie holding the ID.

**Sources:** 01.6 BIR Tax Registration > Documentary requirements: sole proprietor (BIR checklist F1101, revised July 2025); 01.6 BIR Tax Registration > Which form; 01.6 BIR Tax Registration > After registration

## T12: What is the 8% income tax option and who can choose it?

**Expected:** For non-VAT taxpayers with gross sales up to PHP 3 million; 8% on gross sales above PHP 250,000 in lieu of graduated rates and 3% percentage tax.

**Tool:** search_knowledge_base (20.7 s)

If your yearly gross sales are ₱3 million or less and you are not VAT-registered, you can choose the 8% income tax on gross sales in excess of ₱250,000. This replaces both the graduated income tax and the 3% percentage tax. The BIR or an accountant can advise you on which option is better for your situation.

**Sources:** 08.2 Common questions across topics; 01.6 BIR Tax Registration > Income tax options for sole proprietors; 01.6 BIR Tax Registration > Taxpayer size classification (EOPT Act)

## T14: I'm closing my business. What do I need to do with BIR?

**Expected:** File BIR Form 1905 with ending inventory, unused invoices inventory, original COR; otherwise remain liable for returns and penalties.

**Tool:** search_knowledge_base (26.7 s)

To close your business with BIR, you need to file BIR Form 1905. You will also need a list of ending inventory, unused invoices, original BIR notices and permits, and the Certificate of Registration. The documentary stamp tax is ₱30. File at your BIR Revenue District Office in Baguio City and Benguet.

## T34: I have a sari-sari store. Is there a loan for me?

**Expected:** RISE UP Tindahan Micro: up to PHP 300,000 at 10%/yr for sari-sari stores in an accredited FMCG network; or RISE UP micro loan.

**Tool:** search_knowledge_base (38.5 s)

Yes, there is a loan program for you. The RISE UP Tindahan loan program is available for sari-sari stores, retail stores, dealers, and distributors under the network of an SB Corp-accredited consumer-goods (FMCG) company. To be eligible, you must be 100% Filipino-owned (for sole proprietorships and partnerships) or at least 60% Filipino-owned MSME for corporations, with assets of ₱100 million or less (excluding land), and have been participating in the supply chain with an accredited FMCG company for at least 1 year. For loans above ₱1 million, the requirement is 3 years. You must also have no past-due SB Corp accounts.

**Sources:** 18.1 Business-type checklists > Sari-sari store in Baguio City; 04.2 Loans through SB Corporation and partner institutions > SB Corp RISE UP loans [VERIFIED, official SB Corp pages, checked November 17, 2026]; RISE UP TINDAHAN <https://sbcorp.gov.ph/rise-up-tindahan/>
