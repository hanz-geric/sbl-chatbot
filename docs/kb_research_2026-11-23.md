### A1. Section 01.2 — BNRS business name: fills the FOR VALIDATION gaps (updates K008, K009, K010)

Source: https://bnrs.dti.gov.ph/faq

**01.2 DTI Business Name Registration > Renewal periods**
- A business name registration is valid for 5 years from the date of registration.
- **Early renewal:** up to 180 calendar days before expiry.
- **Regular renewal:** within 90 calendar days after expiry.
- **Grace period:** within the next 90 calendar days after the regular period, with a 50% surcharge on the registration fee.
- A business name not renewed within the grace period is cancelled.
- (Status: VERIFIED)

**01.2 DTI Business Name Registration > Cancellation** (replaces K009)
- Cancellation is done at selected DTI offices. Grounds listed by BNRS: the business stopped before expiry; the business was sold or transferred; someone else has prior rights to an identical or confusingly similar name; or the business moved outside its registered territorial scope.
- A business name cannot be transferred to a new owner. The old registration must be cancelled and the new owner registers it again. (The interview also mentioned cancellation after the owner's death.)
- (Status: VERIFIED, INTERVIEW-DERIVED)

**01.2 DTI Business Name Registration > Amendments** (replaces K010)
- Changing the territorial scope counts as a **new application**: a new certificate and a new 5-year validity.
- Changes of address, owner's name, or civil status are made at a DTI office.
- Typos can be corrected only for a new registration, by emailing bnrshelpdesk@dtiphmail.dti.gov.ph.
- (Status: VERIFIED)

**01.2 DTI Business Name Registration > Names that are not allowed**
- BNRS rejects names that, among other grounds: suggest unlawful or immoral activity; describe the quality of the goods or services; are already registered as a trade name, trademark, or business name; are purely generic words; are the names of other persons; or are deceptive or misleading.
- "Company", "Corporation", or "Incorporated" may be used only by SEC-registered partnerships or corporations. "Cooperative" may be used only by CDA-registered cooperatives.
- (Status: VERIFIED)

**01.2 DTI Business Name Registration > How to pay and get help**
- Payment: GCash, Maya, credit or debit card, over the counter (7-Eleven, Bayad Center), or Landbank Link.BizPortal. No other BNRS payment is needed after the registration fee.
- BNRS support: bnrs.support@dti.gov.ph; text or Viber 0917 595 3930.
- (Status: VERIFIED)

### A2. Section 10 (new) — Hiring employees

Sources: https://www.sss.gov.ph/employer-er/ , https://www.sss.gov.ph/self-employed/ , https://www.philhealth.gov.ph/members/formal/ , https://nwpc.dole.gov.ph/

**10.1 Hiring employees > Overview**
- When a business hires its first employee, it must register as an employer with **SSS**, **PhilHealth**, and **Pag-IBIG**, and follow DOLE wage rules.
- The **Central Business Portal (https://business.gov.ph)** has one employee-reporting module covering SSS, PhilHealth, and Pag-IBIG.
- A self-employed owner who hires workers must also register as an employer and get an SSS Employer Number.
- (Status: VERIFIED)

**10.2 Hiring employees > SSS employer registration**
- SSS coverage of an employer starts on the **first day of operation**.
- Register through the Central Business Portal (no SS Form R-1 or supporting documents needed), through My.SSS online, or over the counter at an SSS branch.
- Report new employees to SSS **within 30 days from their hiring date**. Employees must have their own SS numbers.
- Each month, deduct the employee share from pay and remit it together with the employer share and EC (employees' compensation) contribution, using a Payment Reference Number (PRN).
- Late contributions are charged a **2% penalty per month** until paid.
- The contribution table in effect since January 1, 2025 is published on the SSS website (https://www.sss.gov.ph/sss-contribution-table/). The chatbot should not quote rates; it should point to that page.
- (Status: VERIFIED)

**10.3 Hiring employees > SSS for self-employed owners**
- A self-employed member is someone who has no employer other than themself.
- Contributions are paid monthly or quarterly using a PRN from My.SSS. The deadline is the **last day of the month after** the month being paid for.
- (Status: VERIFIED)

**10.4 Hiring employees > PhilHealth**
- Workers in the private sector with an employer–employee relationship are covered under PhilHealth's formal economy program. The member submits a PhilHealth Member Registration Form.
- Employer registration steps and premium rates: not verified (PhilHealth contribution table PDF not yet read). Refer users to PhilHealth.
- (Status: VERIFIED, PENDING)

**10.5 Hiring employees > Minimum wage in the Cordillera (CAR)**
- Private sector: **₱505.00 per day** under **Wage Order No. CAR-24**, effective **December 30, 2025**.
- Domestic workers (kasambahay): **₱6,600.00 per month** under **Wage Order No. CAR-DW-07**, effective December 30, 2025.
- Source: National Wages and Productivity Commission (https://nwpc.dole.gov.ph/). Wage orders change; tell users to check the NWPC page for the current order.
- (Status: VERIFIED)

### A3. Section 03.5 — DTI programs: details from dti.gov.ph (adds to K067–K069)

Source hub: https://www.dti.gov.ph/dti-business-center/dti-programs-projects

**03.5 DTI programs > Shared Service Facilities (SSF)**
- DTI provides machines, equipment, tools, and systems that are housed by a **cooperator** and shared with MSMEs to raise productivity and quality.
- Cooperators can be government entities (LGUs, state universities, tech-voc schools), private entities (cooperatives, associations, NGOs, people's organizations, corporations, private schools), or One Person Corporations. Individual MSMEs use the facility; they do not own it.
- Projects support priority industry clusters, product quality, microenterprises, and OTOP products.
- Implemented by DTI regional and provincial offices. National contact: SSF Project Management Office, DTI-BSMED, BSMED@dti.gov.ph, (02) 7791-3310.
- Source: https://www.dti.gov.ph/dti-business-center/dti-programs-projects/dti-shared-service-facilities/
- (Status: VERIFIED)

**03.5 DTI programs > OTOP Next Gen**
- OTOP Next Gen helps MSMEs that already have a "minimum viable product" improve quality, design, packaging, standards compliance, and marketability.
- Contact: otop@dti.gov.ph or the local DTI office.
- Source: https://www.dti.gov.ph/dti-business-center/dti-programs-projects/dti-otop-philippines
- (Status: VERIFIED)

**03.5 DTI programs > Kapatid Mentor ME: who can join**
- A 10-module mentoring program run by DTI and the Philippine Center for Entrepreneurship, where large companies mentor micro and small entrepreneurs.
- Applicants: at least 18; Filipino resident; owner, co-owner, or partner of a registered business operating at least 1 year; has employees or manages a team; willing to attend all sessions and sign a Contract of Commitment; with three references. One mentee per enterprise and per family.
- Online sessions need a computer or phone, stable internet, a headset, and a Facebook account.
- Apply through the nearest Negosyo Center or DTI office.
- Source: https://www.dti.gov.ph/dti-business-center/dti-programs-projects/dti-kapatid-mentor-me-project/
- (Status: VERIFIED)

**03.5 DTI programs > Youth Entrepreneurship Program (YEP)**
- Based on the Youth Entrepreneurship Act (RA 10679). For aspiring and existing youth entrepreneurs aged 18–30.
- Three parts: **Youth Start** (mindset seminars, help with business registration, skills training); **Youth Net** (mentoring, youth entrepreneur organizations); **Youth Match** (market promotion, access to funding, machines and equipment).
- Apply through a DTI office or Negosyo Center.
- Source: https://www.dti.gov.ph/dti-business-center/dti-programs-projects/dti-youth-entrepreneurship-program-yep/
- (Status: VERIFIED)

**03.3 Negosyo Centers > Legal basis**
- Negosyo Centers are set up under the Go Negosyo Act (RA 10644) to promote ease of doing business and help MSMEs access services. They are located in DTI offices, LGUs, schools, NGOs, and malls.
- Source: https://www.dti.gov.ph/dti-business-center/dti-programs-projects/dti-negosyo-center/
- (Status: VERIFIED)

### A5. Section 04.5 (new) — Loans for farm-based businesses

**04.5 Agricultural loans > KAYA (young agripreneurs)**
- DA-ACPC's Kapital Access for Young Agripreneurs: up to **₱500,000**, **0% interest** (the lending partner may charge a service fee up to 3.5%), **no collateral**, payable up to 5 years.
- For agri-fishery entrepreneurs aged **18 to 30** who are graduates of agriculture or fishery courses or programs (formal or non-formal).
- Requirements: government ID, loan application form, simple business plan, proof of RSBSA or FFEDIS registration.
- Apply by signing up at https://access.acpc.gov.ph. Applications are passed to a partner lender (cooperative, cooperative bank, or rural bank).
- Source: https://acpc.gov.ph/kapital-access-for-young-agripreneurs-kaya/
- (Status: VERIFIED)

**04.5 Agricultural loans > SURE (after calamities)**
- DA-ACPC's Survival and Recovery loan: up to **₱25,000**, 0% interest (up to 3% service fee), no collateral, payable up to 3 years.
- For small farmers and fisherfolk in areas under a **state of calamity**, enrolled in RSBSA. Borrowers are identified and endorsed by the DA regional office and Municipal Agriculture Office; no direct application.
- Source: https://acpc.gov.ph/survival-and-recovery-program/
- (Status: VERIFIED)

**04.5 Agricultural loans > DBP AgriNegosyo**
- DA-ACPC and DBP loan for farmers, fisherfolk, and micro and small enterprises registered with SEC, DTI, or CDA.
- Up to 90% of the project cost: ₱100,000 to ₱500,000 for individual farmers; ₱500,000 to ₱5 million for farmer-enterprises. Term loan up to 10 years including up to 2 years grace. Service fee 3.5%. Interest rate not stated on the page.
- Individual farmers must be RSBSA-registered and trained for the project.
- Source: https://www.dbp.ph/developmental-banking/micro-small-and-medium-enterprises/dbp-agrinegosyo-loan-program/
- (Status: VERIFIED)

### A6. Section 01.4 — CDA cooperatives (adds to K013)

### A7. Section 11 (new) — Customer discounts

Source: RA 10754, text at https://ncda.gov.ph/disability-laws/republic-acts/republic-act-no-10754-an-act-expanding-the-benefits-and-privileges-of-persons-with-disability-pwd/

**11.1 PWD discount: what businesses must give**
- Persons with disabilities get **at least 20% discount and VAT exemption** (if applicable) on goods and services for their exclusive use, including: hotels, restaurants, and recreation centers; admission to theaters, cinemas, and similar venues; medicines in drugstores; medical and dental services; domestic air and sea fares; land transport fares; and funeral and burial services.
- Proof: a PWD ID issued by the city or municipal mayor or barangay captain, the PWD's passport, or a transportation discount ID from the national council.
- The PWD discount cannot be combined with a higher discount or another discount program.
- **For the business:** the discount (except government medical services) can be claimed as a **tax deduction** based on the net cost of goods sold or services rendered, with proper documentation.
- (Status: VERIFIED)
