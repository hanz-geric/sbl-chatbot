## T01: Is DTI business name registration enough for me to start operating a business?

**Expected:** No. DTI registers only the business name; you also need a business (Mayor's) permit from the LGU and BIR registration.

**Tool:** search_knowledge_base (18.1 s)

No. DTI registration covers only your business name. You still need a business permit (Mayor’s Permit) from your city or municipality, and BIR registration. Contact the Business Permit Office for more information.

## T09: When should a new business register with the BIR?

**Expected:** On or before start of business: first sale or 30 days after DTI/SEC certificate or Mayor's Permit is issued, whichever comes first.

**Tool:** search_knowledge_base (27.7 s)

A new business must register with the BIR on or before the start of business. The start of business is counted from the first sale, or 30 calendar days after the DTI business name certificate, SEC certificate, or Mayor’s Permit is issued, whichever comes first. You can register online through ORUS or at the Philippine Business Hub or New Business Registration counter of the Revenue District Office (RDO) covering your business address.

## T10: What BIR form does a sole proprietor use to register, and what documents are needed?

**Expected:** BIR Form 1901; government ID; invoices (BIR Printed Invoices or sample from accredited printer); DTI certificate if applicable; PHP 30 DST.

**Tool:** search_knowledge_base (30.5 s)

A sole proprietor uses BIR Form 1901 to register. The required documents are:

*   BIR Form 1901 (two originals for manual applications)
*   One photocopy of a government-issued ID showing name, address, and birthdate.
*   Invoices: either buy BIR Printed Invoices, or submit one final sample of your own invoices printed by an accredited printer.

The documentary stamp tax is ₱30.

**Sources:** 01.6 BIR Tax Registration > Documentary requirements: sole proprietor (BIR checklist F1101, revised July 2025); 01.6 BIR Tax Registration > Which form; 01.6 BIR Tax Registration > Documentary requirements: sole proprietor (BIR checklist F1101, revised July 2025); 01.6 BIR Tax Registration > Moving your business to another RDO (e.g. Baguio City to La Trinidad); 14.2 Online selling > How online sellers register with BIR

## T12: What is the 8% income tax option and who can choose it?

**Expected:** For non-VAT taxpayers with gross sales up to PHP 3 million; 8% on gross sales above PHP 250,000 in lieu of graduated rates and 3% percentage tax.

**Tool:** search_knowledge_base (28.4 s)

If your yearly gross sales are ₱3 million or less and you are not VAT-registered, you can choose the 8% income tax on gross sales in excess of ₱250,000. ## T14: I'm closing my business. What do I need to do with BIR?

**Expected:** File BIR Form 1905 with ending inventory, unused invoices inventory, original COR; otherwise remain liable for returns and penalties.

**Tool:** search_knowledge_base (31.2 s)

## T17: What documents does the Baguio eBPLS portal ask for when applying for a new business permit?

**Expected:** Proof of ownership (or consent to use), building or occupancy permit, parking sketch or affidavit, floor plan, location sketch; if leasing: lease contract, lessor's permit, authorization.

**Tool:** search_knowledge_base (33.3 s)

The Baguio eBPLS portal requires the following documents for a new business permit application:

1.  Proof of ownership of the business location: any one of title, tax declaration, waiver of rights, deed of sale, notice of award, or a notarized authorization or consent to use the property.
2.  Building permit or occupancy permit.
3.  Parking: a simple sketch plan with dimensions, or a notarized affidavit of undertaking if the premises has no adequate parking.
4.  Floor plan or simple sketch plan with dimensions.
5.  Sketch of location.
6.  If leasing: a notarized contract of lease, the lessor’s business permit, and a notarized authorization to process.

## V19: What do I need to open a carinderia in Baguio?

**Expected:** DTI name, barangay clearance, health certificates and sanitary permit (CHSO), Mayor's Permit, BIR; SSS etc. if hiring.

**Tool:** search_knowledge_base (35.9 s)

To open a carinderia in Baguio City, you will need the following:

1.  Register the business name with DTI.
2.  Get the barangay business clearance.
3.  Get health certificates for everyone who will handle food and the sanitary permit/clearance from the City Health Services Office.
4.  Apply for the Mayor’s Permit, along with other clearances such as fire safety.
5.  Register with BIR on or before the first sale.
6.  If you hire workers, register with SSS, PhilHealth, and Pag-IBIG and pay at least the CAR minimum wage.
7.  If selling packaged food products, you may also need FDA registration.
