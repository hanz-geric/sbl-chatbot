## T01: Is DTI business name registration enough for me to start operating a business?

**Expected:** No. DTI registers only the business name; you also need a business (Mayor's) permit from the LGU and BIR registration.

**Tool:** search_knowledge_base (48.7 s)

No. DTI registration covers only your business name. You still need a business permit (Mayor’s Permit) from your city or municipality, and BIR registration. Please contact the Business Permits Office for more information.

**Sources:** 01.9 Common Questions – Business Registration; 02.8 Common questions – business permits; 01.2 DTI Business Name Registration (Sole Proprietorship) > Validity and renewal

## T10: What BIR form does a sole proprietor use to register, and what documents are needed?

**Expected:** BIR Form 1901; government ID; invoices (BIR Printed Invoices or sample from accredited printer); DTI certificate if applicable; PHP 30 DST.

**Tool:** search_knowledge_base (34.2 s)

A sole proprietor uses BIR Form 1901 to register. The required documents are:

*   BIR Form 1901 (two originals for manual applications)
*   One photocopy of a government-issued ID
*   Invoices (BIR Printed Invoices or a sample printed by an accredited printer)

You can also include a DTI business name certificate, Special Power of Attorney, BMBE Certificate of Authority, work visa, or franchise documents as applicable.

**Sources:** 01.6 BIR Tax Registration > Documentary requirements: sole proprietor (BIR checklist F1101, revised July 2025); 01.6 BIR Tax Registration > Which form; 01.6 BIR Tax Registration > After registration

## T12: What is the 8% income tax option and who can choose it?

**Expected:** For non-VAT taxpayers with gross sales up to PHP 3 million; 8% on gross sales above PHP 250,000 in lieu of graduated rates and 3% percentage tax.

**Tool:** search_knowledge_base (25.1 s)

If your yearly gross sales are ₱3 million or less and you are not VAT-registered, you can choose the 8% income tax on gross sales in excess of ₱250,000. This replaces both the graduated income tax and the 3% percentage tax. The ₱250,000 reduction applies only to those earning purely from business, not mixed-income earners. Ask the BIR or an accountant to determine which option is better for you.

**Sources:** 08.2 Common questions across topics; 01.6 BIR Tax Registration > Income tax options for sole proprietors; 01.6 BIR Tax Registration > Taxpayer size classification (EOPT Act)
